## Overview
Dr. BRC's Health Diet Plan is designed to support the body's natural healing and recovery processes through balanced nutrition, appropriate hydration, restorative sleep, and gentle mobility work. The plan is suitable for adults recovering from non-critical illness, surgery (with physician approval), mild-to-moderate injury recovery, or anyone seeking a structured recovery-oriented wellness plan.

Disclaimer: This plan is educational and not a substitute for professional medical advice. Individuals with medical conditions, pregnancy, or those taking prescription medications should consult their physician before starting any new diet or recovery program.

## Goals
- Provide nutrient-dense meals that support repair and immune function.
- Maintain stable blood sugar and steady energy throughout the day.
- Promote anti-inflammatory foods and adequate protein for tissue repair.
- Encourage sleep, stress reduction, and safe gradual mobility exercises.

## Daily Diet Plan (General Template)
Note: Portion sizes should be adapted to age, sex, activity level, and clinical needs.

- Breakfast (within 1 hour of waking)
  - High-quality protein (2 servings): e.g., 2 eggs or 150 g Greek yogurt or a plant-based protein portion
  - Complex carbohydrates: 1 serving whole-grain toast, oats, or cooked millet
  - Fruit: 1 small serving (berries preferred for antioxidants)
  - Healthy fat: 1 tablespoon nuts/seeds or 1/2 avocado
  - Beverage: warm water with lemon or herbal tea

- Mid-morning (optional light snack)
  - Small handful of nuts or a piece of fruit + 1 oz cheese or a protein-rich snack

- Lunch
  - Lean protein: poultry, fish, legumes, or tofu (approx. palm-sized portion)
  - Mixed vegetables (steamed or salad) focusing on colorful choices
  - Whole grains or starchy vegetable: brown rice, quinoa, sweet potato (small to moderate portion)
  - Healthy fat: olive oil-based dressing or olives

- Afternoon (light snack)
  - Vegetable sticks with hummus or low-sugar yogurt + berries

- Dinner (light, easily digested)
  - Lean protein (smaller portion than lunch)
  - Cooked vegetables or soup
  - Small portion of complex carbohydrate if needed for energy
  - Avoid heavy fried foods or very late large meals

- Hydration
  - Aim for 2–3 liters/day depending on body size and clinical needs
  - Prefer water, electrolyte-balanced fluids if recovering from dehydration
  - Limit sugary drinks and caffeine (moderate tea/coffee ok)

- Supplements (consider with clinician)
  - Protein supplement if intake is inadequate
  - Vitamin D, omega-3 (fish oil), and a general multivitamin as indicated by blood tests
  - Probiotics for gut-support when indicated

## Special Considerations
- Anti-inflammatory emphasis: include fatty fish (salmon, mackerel), turmeric, ginger, berries, leafy greens, and nuts.
- Blood sugar control: prefer low glycemic index carbs, combine carbs with protein & fat to stabilize glucose.
- Allergies & intolerances: substitute appropriate alternatives (e.g., dairy -> fortified plant yogurt).

## Body Curing & Recovery Methods
These methods are complementary to the diet and support tissue healing, functional recovery, and return to baseline activity.

1. Rest & Sleep
   - Prioritize 7–9 hours of sleep per night; establish consistent sleep/wake times.
   - Napping: short naps (20–30 min) if needed, avoid long daytime sleep that disrupts night sleep.

2. Gradual Mobility & Rehabilitation
   - Begin with gentle range-of-motion exercises and short walks as tolerated.
   - Progressively increase duration and intensity based on pain, swelling, and clinician guidance.
   - Recommended: 2–3 short mobility sessions per day (5–15 minutes) for early recovery.

3. Breathing & Relaxation
   - Daily diaphragmatic breathing or paced breathing (5–10 minutes) to reduce stress and support vagal tone.
   - Mindfulness, progressive muscle relaxation, or guided imagery to help with pain coping and sleep.

4. Pain & Inflammation Management (non-pharmacologic)
   - Ice for acute swelling (first 48–72 hours) in short intervals; heat for stiff muscles after acute phase.
   - Compression and elevation for limb swelling when applicable.
   - Gentle massage or myofascial release from a licensed practitioner when appropriate.

5. Progressive Strengthening
   - Once pain and inflammation reduce, introduce light resistance exercises focusing on form and controlled loading.
   - Aim for 2–3 strength sessions/week depending on recovery stage.

6. Monitoring & Safe Progression
   - Track sleep, pain levels, swelling, appetite, bowel movements, and energy.
   - Use a simple daily checklist or recovery log to note improvements or setbacks.
   - If signs of infection, worsening pain, new neurological symptoms, chest pain, or other red flags occur, seek immediate medical attention.

## Contraindications & When to Seek Medical Care
- This plan is NOT for acute critical conditions without medical oversight.
- People with uncontrolled diabetes, severe cardiovascular disease, advanced kidney disease, active cancer treatment, pregnancy (without consultation), or immunosuppression should consult their treating clinician before following this plan.
- Seek urgent care for fever with wound issues, increasing redness, drainage, severe or worsening pain, shortness of breath, chest pain, or neurological changes.
